How it works

Like the Dock, but for your chats.

No windows to manage, no tabs to lose. Three steps and it's part of the screen.

1
Download and drag
Open the DMG, drop SidePiece in Applications, open it. Sign in to Telegram Web once; the session stays logged in across quits.
2
Forget it's there
A thin strip pins itself to the left or right screen edge. It stays out of your way while you work.
3
Hover, and it slides out
Rest your cursor on the strip for a beat and the panel glides out. It ignores drive-by brushes on the way to a scrollbar.
Features

Small panel, full Telegram.

Everything the beta does today, no filler.

Pin it open when you need it
Keep Panel Open holds the panel in place while you type. Unpin from the menu bar and it docks itself again.
Unread counts everywhere
A red pill on the strip, a badge on the Dock icon, and a count in the menu bar, plus optional native notifications.
Your side, your width
Pick the left or right edge, set the panel width, and switch on compact mode from the menu bar.
Polite with your links
Links in chats open in your normal browser. The panel itself only ever loads web.telegram.org.
Wakes you when it matters
Turn on expand-on-notification and the panel slides out on its own when a message arrives. Off by default.
Launch at Login, on your terms
There's an explicit toggle in the menu bar, and it's off until you turn it on. Nothing starts behind your back.

Privacy

Your chats stay yours.

🔒SidePiece loads Telegram Web. The Mac build checks GitHub daily for updates and sends anonymous install and active-use pings; the Windows beta does not include those pings or automatic updates. No chat contents are sent to us. Your Telegram Web sign-in stays on your device. When reporting a bug, never send screenshots of private chats.
